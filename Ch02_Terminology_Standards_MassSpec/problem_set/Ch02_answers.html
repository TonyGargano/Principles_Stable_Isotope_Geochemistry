Answer 7

εa–b = 1000(α − 1) = 1000·((1000 + δa)/(1000 + δb) − 1)reconstructed

If δb = 0 and δa = 10, then ε is exactly equal to 10. If, for example, δb = 100 and δa = 110, then α = 1110/1100 = 1.00909 and ε = 9.09. But δa − δb = 10.

Answer 9

There are two sulfur atoms in the pyrite formula (FeS2) and only one in sphalerite. Therefore we write the reaction as follows:

Fe0.532S + Zn34S = Fe0.534S + Zn32S

In terms of thermodynamic properties such as volume or free energy, we would use 1/2 the value reported for FeS2, because our formula is written as 1/2 of the total formula.

Answer 13

The definition assumes that there is more than one isotope of a particular element. In the case of fluorine, for example, there is only one naturally occurring isotope. There is no requirement that two isotopes of an element must exist.

Answer 14

Starting with the definition of delta and knowing that D/H of VSMOW = 155.76·10−6, we determine the D/H ratio of our starting water (δD = −90‰) to be 141.73·10−6, and the D/H ratio of water with δD = +500‰ to be 233.63·10−6. For one liter, the amount of D2O is 0.234 ml per liter of water. The same calculation for our starting water gives 0.1417 ml per liter of water. The difference is 0.092 ml. That is, adding only ~0.09 ml of D2O to a liter of water with an initial δD of −90‰ will increase the δD value to +500‰ vs VSMOW.
